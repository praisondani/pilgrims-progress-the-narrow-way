# The Pilgrim’s Progress — Story-to-Game Scene Bible
## Narrative, Levels, Objectives, Interactions, Symbols, and Art Direction

**Purpose:** This document translates John Bunyan’s *The Pilgrim’s Progress, Part One* into a scene-by-scene structure suitable for an interactive 3D browser game.

**Important adaptation note:** Editions divide the work differently. The game should organize levels around the story’s major episodes rather than depending on a particular publisher’s chapter numbering.

**Narrative principle:** Preserve the essential sequence, characters, choices, warnings, and destination while adapting prose into interactive experiences.

---

# Part I — MVP Journey

## Level 0 — The Dream

### Original story event

The narrator rests in a den and dreams of a man in rags carrying a book and a great burden.

### Player experience

The player briefly controls the Dreamer, explores the wilderness, lies down, and enters the dream.

### Purpose

- Establish the allegorical frame.
- Tell the player that places and characters carry symbolic meaning.
- Introduce basic movement and inspection.

### Interaction ideas

- Light a lantern.
- Inspect the book.
- Listen to wind carrying indistinct words.
- Lie down to begin.

### Story explanation

The adventure is presented as a dream about a spiritual journey from destruction to eternal life.

### Transition

The camera dives into the open book and emerges inside Christian’s home.

---

## Level 1 — The City of Destruction

### Original story event

Christian reads a book, becomes aware of judgment, and feels a heavy burden. His family does not believe him. He fears the destruction of the city and asks what he must do.

### Main characters

- Christian
- Christian’s wife
- Christian’s children
- Townspeople

### Player goal

Understand Christian’s crisis and choose to leave the city.

### Scene sequence

1. Wake in Christian’s house.
2. Find and read the Book.
3. Watch the burden form.
4. Attempt to continue normal household tasks.
5. Speak with the family.
6. Explore the city.
7. See signs that the city is unstable.
8. Hear or glimpse Evangelist beyond the city.
9. Leave through the outskirts.

### Interactive storytelling

Before reading:

- Christian walks normally.
- Colors are dull but stable.

After reading:

- Burden appears.
- The city’s sounds become oppressive.
- NPC voices feel distant.
- Some buildings subtly lean.
- The path out becomes visually brighter.

### Optional interactions

- Comfort a child.
- Look back at the home.
- Tell a citizen what was read.
- Refuse market distractions.
- Inspect locked city gates or warning notices.

### Core symbol

**City of Destruction:** The doomed world and a life continuing without regard for judgment.

### Failure handling

The player may delay, explore, or return to the doorway, but cannot permanently abandon the journey. Narration redirects without shaming the player.

### End condition

Christian crosses the field beyond the city.

---

## Level 2 — Evangelist, Obstinate, and Pliable

### Original story event

Evangelist directs Christian toward a shining light and the Wicket Gate. Obstinate tries to make him return. Pliable briefly joins him.

### Main characters

- Evangelist
- Obstinate
- Pliable

### Player goal

Choose the journey despite social pressure and follow the distant light.

### Scene sequence

1. Christian runs from the city.
2. Obstinate and Pliable catch him.
3. Dialogue confrontation.
4. Obstinate leaves in anger.
5. Pliable joins after hearing about the promised destination.
6. Evangelist’s guidance becomes the objective.
7. Christian and Pliable enter marshland.

### Interactive choices

Responses may express:

- Conviction
- Fear
- uncertainty
- compassion
- eagerness

These affect dialogue tone but not the core route.

### Core symbols

- **Evangelist:** Faithful proclamation and guidance.
- **Obstinate:** Refusal to consider the truth.
- **Pliable:** Quick enthusiasm without rooted endurance.
- **Shining light:** Direction based on revealed truth.

### End condition

The ground collapses into the Slough of Despond.

---

## Level 3 — The Slough of Despond

### Original story event

Christian and Pliable fall into a bog formed by fears, doubts, and discouragement. Pliable escapes toward home. Christian struggles under his burden until Help pulls him out.

### Main characters

- Christian
- Pliable
- Help

### Player goal

Cross the bog while burdened.

### Scene sequence

1. Sudden fall into mud.
2. Learn mud movement.
3. Pliable complains.
4. Build or discover a temporary route.
5. Pliable turns back.
6. Fog obscures the path.
7. Christian sinks near the far edge.
8. Help reaches down and rescues him.

### Gameplay metaphors

- Moving directly toward panic causes deeper mud.
- Pausing and observing reveals firmer steps.
- Abandoned boards and stones represent previously provided guidance.
- The burden increases sinking.

### Core symbol

**Slough of Despond:** Discouragement produced by awareness of sin, fear, and unresolved guilt.

### Story explanation

The route out exists, but a burdened traveler may fail to see or use it.

### End condition

Help places Christian on firm ground.

---

## Level 4 — Mr. Worldly Wiseman

### Original story event

Mr. Worldly Wiseman persuades Christian to seek relief through morality and human respectability instead of continuing to the Wicket Gate.

### Main characters

- Mr. Worldly Wiseman
- Christian
- Evangelist

### Player goal

Experience the attractive wrong path and recognize its danger.

### Scene sequence

1. Meet Worldly Wiseman at a crossroads.
2. Hear him criticize Christian’s route.
3. Follow the beautiful road toward Morality.
4. See the distant village.
5. Approach Mount Sinai.
6. The mountain overhangs the road.
7. Lightning and falling stone create danger.
8. Christian despairs.
9. Evangelist returns and corrects him.
10. Return to the path toward the Wicket Gate.

### Core symbols

- **Worldly Wiseman:** Advice that values comfort, reputation, and self-reliance over truth.
- **Village of Morality:** The promise of moral improvement without inward transformation.
- **Mount Sinai:** The law exposing guilt but unable to remove the burden.

### Adaptation rule

Do not caricature Worldly Wiseman as obviously evil. He should appear intelligent, calm, respectable, and persuasive.

### End condition

Christian sees the Wicket Gate in the distance.

---

## Level 5 — The Wicket Gate

### Original story event

Christian reaches the narrow gate. Goodwill pulls him inside because arrows are being fired from a nearby stronghold.

### Main characters

- Christian
- Goodwill

### Player goal

Reach the gate, knock, and enter.

### Scene sequence

1. Cross an exposed approach.
2. Avoid arrows.
3. Read the words over the gate.
4. Knock.
5. Knock again.
6. Goodwill opens the gate and pulls Christian through.
7. Christian explains his journey.
8. Goodwill directs him to the Interpreter’s House.

### Core symbols

- **Wicket Gate:** Entrance to the appointed way.
- **Narrow approach:** The difficulty of entering against opposition.
- **Arrows:** Spiritual hostility and distraction.
- **Goodwill:** Welcoming mercy.

### End condition

Christian leaves the gate’s courtyard on the narrow path.

---

## Level 6 — The Interpreter’s House

### Original story event

The Interpreter shows Christian a series of symbolic scenes to prepare him for the journey.

### Main characters

- Interpreter
- symbolic figures in each room

### Player goal

Complete each lesson room and understand its meaning.

---

### Room 1 — The Portrait

A grave and noble portrait depicts a true spiritual guide.

**Interaction:** Manipulate light to reveal the portrait.

**Meaning:** A faithful guide looks toward heaven, carries truth, and leaves worldly concerns behind.

---

### Room 2 — The Dusty Parlor

A person sweeps a dusty room, making the dust worse. A maiden sprinkles water and the room becomes clean.

**Interaction:** Open the room, observe failed cleaning, bring or release water.

**Meaning:** The law reveals and stirs sin; the gospel cleanses the heart.

---

### Room 3 — Passion and Patience

Passion demands good things immediately and quickly wastes them. Patience waits for lasting good.

**Interaction:** Observe the transformation of their possessions over time.

**Meaning:** Immediate worldly rewards fade; eternal reward endures.

---

### Room 4 — The Fire by the Wall

A fire continues burning even while someone pours water on it. Behind the wall, another secretly feeds it oil.

**Interaction:** Investigate both sides of the wall.

**Meaning:** Grace can sustain faith despite visible opposition.

---

### Room 5 — The Palace and the Warrior

Armed opponents block entry to a glorious palace. A brave man fights through them and enters.

**Interaction:** Take symbolic armor and advance using timing, defense, and courage.

**Meaning:** The journey requires resolve and perseverance.

---

### Room 6 — The Iron Cage

A despairing man sits in an iron cage and explains his condition.

**Interaction:** Speak and inspect the cage.

**Meaning:** A severe warning about hardening, apostasy, and despair.

**Tone:** Solemn, restrained, and non-exploitative.

---

### Room 7 — The Troubled Dream

A man dreams of judgment and awakens terrified.

**Interaction:** Walk through an abstract vision.

**Meaning:** The urgency of being prepared.

---

### Exit

The Interpreter blesses Christian and sends him onward.

### End condition

The house doors open onto a road leading uphill toward the Cross.

---

## Level 7 — The Cross and the Open Grave

### Original story event

Christian reaches a Cross. His burden falls from his shoulders, rolls down a hill, and disappears into an open grave. Three Shining Ones greet him, give him new clothing, mark him, and provide a sealed roll.

### Main characters

- Christian
- Three Shining Ones

### Player goal

Reach the Cross and receive the signs of a changed state.

### Scene sequence

1. Climb the final hill.
2. Burden becomes almost unbearable.
3. Reach the Cross.
4. Burden breaks free.
5. Burden rolls into the grave.
6. Christian responds with relief and wonder.
7. Shining Ones replace his rags.
8. Receive the seal and roll.
9. Explore the overlook.
10. View the road ahead.

### Core symbols

- **Cross:** Christ’s saving work.
- **Open grave:** The burden is removed and not recovered.
- **New clothing:** Righteousness and a changed standing.
- **Mark:** Identification and assurance.
- **Sealed roll:** Testimony and proof to be presented at the destination.

### MVP ending

The player looks through a viewing instrument or magical map and sees:

- Hill Difficulty
- Palace Beautiful
- Valley of Humiliation
- distant Vanity Fair
- Doubting Castle
- Delectable Mountains
- Celestial City

Fade to title and credits.

# Part II — Full Journey Roadmap

## Level 8 — Simple, Sloth, and Presumption

### Original story event

Christian encounters three sleeping men bound near the road. He tries to warn them, but they refuse to respond appropriately.

### Gameplay

- Inspect their bonds.
- Attempt to wake each man.
- Hear distinct excuses.
- Learn that not every NPC can be forced to accept help.

### Symbols

- **Simple:** Lack of understanding
- **Sloth:** Refusal to act
- **Presumption:** False confidence without vigilance

---

## Level 9 — Formalist and Hypocrisy

### Original story event

Two men climb over the wall instead of entering through the gate. They claim their tradition is sufficient.

### Gameplay

- Travel alongside them.
- Observe shortcuts that bypass required entry.
- Dialogue-based discernment challenge.
- Their routes later diverge into danger.

### Symbols

- **Formalist:** External religion
- **Hypocrisy:** Appearance without reality

---

## Level 10 — Hill Difficulty

### Original story event

The path divides at a steep hill. Christian takes the difficult upward path while Formalist and Hypocrisy choose easier routes.

### Gameplay

- Sustained climbing traversal.
- Manage stamina without the former burden.
- Read signs carefully.
- Witness the consequences of easier side roads.

### Environment

- steep stone steps
- waterfalls
- cliff paths
- shifting weather
- broad views back toward the Cross

### Lesson

The right road may be difficult without being wrong.

---

## Level 11 — The Arbor and the Lost Roll

### Original story event

Christian rests at an arbor, falls asleep, and loses his sealed roll. He later realizes the loss and must return.

### Gameplay

1. Reach a safe rest location.
2. Optional journal review.
3. Dreamlike sleep transition.
4. Continue upward.
5. At a checkpoint, discover the roll is missing.
6. Backtrack while daylight fades.
7. Search the arbor.
8. Recover the roll.

### Design purpose

Teach that carelessness can cause costly backtracking without permanently ending the journey.

---

## Level 12 — Timorous and Mistrust

### Original story event

Two frightened travelers warn Christian that lions are ahead and retreat.

### Gameplay

- Hear exaggerated danger reports.
- Decide whether to continue.
- Observe distant lions.
- Use discernment rather than immediate retreat.

### Symbols

- **Timorous:** Fear
- **Mistrust:** Lack of confidence in the promised way

---

## Level 13 — Palace Beautiful

### Original story event

Christian reaches a palace where Watchful, Discretion, Prudence, Piety, and Charity receive him. He tells his story, rests, sees the records and armory, and is equipped.

### Main characters

- Watchful
- Discretion
- Prudence
- Piety
- Charity

### Gameplay structure

A safe narrative hub:

- Arrival and examination at the gate
- Shared meal
- Conversation choices
- Story recap
- Explore library and records
- Visit armory
- Select symbolic equipment
- Sleep and morning departure

### Equipment

- armor
- sword
- shield
- helmet
- suitable footwear

These should support later encounters without turning the game into a loot RPG.

### Symbol

**Palace Beautiful:** Fellowship, teaching, hospitality, and preparation.

---

## Level 14 — Valley of Humiliation

### Original story event

Christian descends into a low valley and confronts Apollyon, who tries to make him turn back. A fierce battle follows.

### Main characters

- Christian
- Apollyon

### Gameplay

This is the game’s first major boss encounter.

Phases:

1. Dialogue temptation
2. intimidation
3. defensive combat
4. Christian loses footing
5. recover the sword
6. final resistance
7. Apollyon retreats

### Combat principles

- Focus on defense, timing, truth-based responses, and perseverance.
- Avoid gore.
- Use symbolic wounds and damaged armor.
- Dialogue choices during combat can restore resolve or expose deception.

### Symbol

**Apollyon:** Accusation, temptation, domination, and opposition.

---

## Level 15 — Valley of the Shadow of Death

### Original story event

Christian crosses a terrifying valley filled with darkness, a ditch, a dangerous path, strange sounds, and spiritual threats.

### Gameplay genre

Atmospheric survival and navigation.

Mechanics:

- limited visibility
- listen for path direction
- use spoken prayer or remembered passages
- avoid false lights
- maintain composure
- no traditional combat required

### Environment

- narrow path between ditch and mire
- caves
- skeletal trees
- fire and smoke
- distant voices
- darkness that reacts to the player

### End

Dawn reveals dangers Christian passed without fully seeing them.

---

## Level 16 — Faithful

### Original story event

Christian catches up with Faithful. They compare their journeys. Faithful recounts encounters Christian did not experience.

### Main characters in Faithful’s account

- Wanton
- Adam the First
- Moses
- Discontent
- Shame

### Gameplay structure

Use playable memory vignettes.

The player alternates between:

- walking conversation as Christian
- brief scenes played as Faithful

### Purpose

Show that different travelers face different temptations.

---

## Level 17 — Talkative

### Original story event

Christian and Faithful meet Talkative, who speaks impressively about religion but lacks corresponding practice.

### Gameplay

Dialogue investigation:

- Ask questions
- compare claims
- observe contradictions
- let Faithful confront Talkative
- decide what evidence reveals character

### Symbol

**Talkative:** Words without transformed conduct.

---

## Level 18 — Evangelist’s Warning

### Original story event

Evangelist meets Christian and Faithful and warns that suffering awaits them in Vanity Fair.

### Gameplay

- Rest at a roadside fire.
- Review the journey.
- Receive a warning without being told every detail.
- Choose preparations.
- See the lights of Vanity Fair in the distance.

---

## Level 19 — Vanity Fair

### Original story event

A permanent fair sells every kind of worldly object, title, pleasure, and person. Christian and Faithful stand out. They are mocked, beaten, imprisoned, and tried. Faithful is condemned and killed; Christian later escapes, and Hopeful joins him.

### Environment

The largest social scene in the game:

- colorful market
- banners
- performers
- merchants
- courts
- prison
- crowds
- spectacle masking corruption

### Gameplay phases

1. Enter the fair.
2. Resist merchants and distractions.
3. Crowd suspicion rises.
4. Disorder erupts.
5. Arrest.
6. Prison dialogue.
7. Trial.
8. Faithful’s testimony.
9. Condemnation.
10. Symbolic martyrdom, shown with restraint.
11. Faithful is carried upward.
12. Christian escapes.
13. Hopeful joins.

### Major characters

- Faithful
- Hopeful
- Beelzebub’s authorities
- Judge Hategood
- Envy
- Superstition
- Pickthank

### Adaptation caution

Do not make Vanity Fair only a shopping satire. It represents an entire social order hostile to pilgrims who refuse its values.

---

## Level 20 — Hopeful and the Road Beyond Vanity

### Original story event

Hopeful becomes Christian’s companion as a result of Faithful’s witness.

### Gameplay

- Quiet walking section after an intense level.
- Companion commands and cooperative traversal tutorial.
- Hopeful asks questions about Faithful and the journey.
- Player reflects through dialogue.

### Companion system

Hopeful can:

- boost Christian up ledges
- hold mechanisms
- carry a light
- revive after a fall
- comment on locations
- participate in dialogue

---

## Level 21 — By-Ends and His Companions

### Original story event

The pilgrims encounter By-Ends, who adapts religion to convenience and profit.

### Gameplay

- Social dialogue challenge
- identify evasive answers
- choose whether to continue traveling together
- overhear companions rationalizing profit

### Symbols

- **By-Ends:** Religion used for advantage
- **Mr. Money-love and companions:** Rationalized greed

---

## Level 22 — Demas and the Silver Mine

### Original story event

Demas invites the travelers toward a dangerous silver mine. Christian and Hopeful refuse. A nearby pillar warns them.

### Gameplay

- Attractive optional detour
- unstable mine physics
- glittering visual temptation
- Hopeful can be persuaded or cautioned
- inspect the pillar of salt

### Design

The mine may contain collectibles, but taking them should create disadvantages and no essential reward. Avoid teaching the player that temptation is secretly the best loot route.

---

## Level 23 — The River and By-Path Meadow

### Original story event

The road becomes rough. Christian persuades Hopeful to enter the easier By-Path Meadow. A storm comes, and they become lost.

### Gameplay

1. Frustrating rocky path.
2. Visible soft meadow beyond a low barrier.
3. Player chooses the easier path.
4. Meet Vain-confidence.
5. Follow him at dusk.
6. He falls into a hidden pit.
7. Storm floods the return route.
8. Seek shelter.

### Symbol

**By-Path Meadow:** Leaving the appointed path for comfort.

### Adaptation rule

Christian must own the decision. Do not blame Hopeful.

---

## Level 24 — Doubting Castle

### Original story event

Giant Despair captures Christian and Hopeful, imprisons and beats them, and urges them toward self-destruction. They pray, remember the Key of Promise, and escape.

### Main characters

- Giant Despair
- Diffidence
- Christian
- Hopeful

### Tone and safety

This level contains despair and coercion. Present it symbolically and carefully.

Avoid:

- graphic abuse
- detailed self-harm methods
- glamorization
- hopeless framing

Emphasize:

- companionship
- resistance
- prayer
- memory
- escape
- the forgotten key already possessed

### Gameplay

1. Wake in prison.
2. Explore cell.
3. endure time and weather transitions.
4. dialogue between Christian and Hopeful.
5. piece together remembered promises.
6. discover Key of Promise.
7. unlock cell and courtyard gates.
8. stealth escape while Giant Despair sleeps or searches.
9. place a warning monument outside.

### Symbol

- **Giant Despair:** Crushing hopelessness
- **Doubting Castle:** Imprisonment by doubt
- **Key of Promise:** Truth already given but forgotten

---

## Level 25 — Delectable Mountains

Shepherds receive the pilgrims, show them distant sights and warnings, and give directions toward the Celestial City.

### Main characters

- Knowledge
- Experience
- Watchful
- Sincere

### Gameplay

A peaceful exploratory level:

- shepherd conversations
- panoramic overlooks
- telescope interactions
- warning locations
- map update

### Viewpoints

- Hill Error
- Mount Caution

**Delectable Mountains:** Mature guidance, perspective, and warning.

## Level 26 — Ignorance

### Original story event

The pilgrims meet Ignorance, who believes his own sincerity and conduct are sufficient, but he lacks the required foundation.

### Gameplay

Recurring walking dialogue.

Ignorance should:

- be pleasant
- appear confident
- express plausible ideas
- resist correction
- separate and rejoin later

### Adaptation rule

Avoid making him foolish in appearance. His danger is misplaced confidence.

---

## Level 27 — Little-Faith

### Original story event

Christian recounts the story of Little-Faith, who was robbed and wounded but retained the jewels needed for the journey.

### Gameplay

Playable parable vignette:

- ambush
- loss of spending money
- protect essential documents
- escape wounded
- reflect on what was and was not lost

### Purpose

Teach the distinction between severe loss and final loss.

---

## Level 28 — The Flatterer and the Net

### Original story event

A false guide leads the pilgrims away and traps them in a net. A Shining One frees and corrects them.

### Gameplay

- Navigation based on guide behavior
- subtle contradictions in directions
- path gradually loops
- net trap closes
- dialogue reflection
- rescue

### Symbol

**Flatterer:** Deception that gains trust through pleasing speech.

---

## Level 29 — Atheist

### Original story event

Atheist laughs at the pilgrims and claims there is no Celestial City because he failed to find it.

### Gameplay

- Dialogue on a barren road
- environmental perspective puzzle
- the city is not visible from the current low ground
- player chooses whether testimony, map, and prior signs are sufficient to continue

### Adaptation rule

Present the exchange thoughtfully rather than as a shallow caricature.

---

## Level 30 — The Enchanted Ground

### Original story event

The ground causes dangerous sleepiness. Christian and Hopeful keep one another awake through conversation and testimony.

### Gameplay

Cooperative companion survival:

- sleep meter
- environmental lull
- keep moving
- ask Hopeful questions
- tell stories
- activate memory points
- avoid seductive resting places

### Key narrative

Hopeful tells how he came to faith after witnessing Christian and Faithful at Vanity Fair.

### Symbol

**Enchanted Ground:** Spiritual complacency near the end of the journey.

---

## Level 31 — Beulah

### Original story event

The pilgrims enter a beautiful country near the Celestial City. They enjoy gardens, songs, fruit, and a clearer view of their destination.

### Gameplay

- No threats
- free exploration
- collect final journal entries
- speak with inhabitants
- hear bells
- see the city clearly
- review the whole journey
- prepare to cross the river

### Art direction

The most beautiful earthly region:

- orchards
- vineyards
- flowers
- golden evening
- distant city above clouds
- music in the air

### Symbol

**Beulah:** Foretaste, nearness, assurance, and rest before the final crossing.

---

## Level 32 — The River of Death

### Original story event

Christian and Hopeful must cross a river with no bridge. Christian becomes afraid and sinks deeply, while Hopeful encourages him. They reach the other side.

### Gameplay

This is an emotional traversal sequence, not a swimming simulator.

Mechanics:

- water depth reflects Christian’s fear
- Hopeful remains close
- remembered promises create visible footing or light
- dialogue choices help Christian continue
- the player cannot turn back into Beulah indefinitely

### Accessibility

Provide:

- story-only mode
- reduced underwater visuals
- no breath timer
- no drowning animation
- checkpoint at river entry

### Symbol

**River:** Death and the final passage.

---

## Level 33 — The Celestial City

### Original story event

Shining Ones escort the pilgrims upward. The city welcomes them with music, bells, and glory. They present their certificates and enter.

### Gameplay

1. Emerge from the river in changed clothing.
2. Ascend the mountain road.
3. Join a growing procession.
4. Hear greetings from across the landscape.
5. Present the sealed roll.
6. Gates open.
7. Enter the city.
8. Experience a final interactive celebration.
9. View recap of people, choices, and lessons.
10. End the dream and return to the Dreamer.

### Visual direction

Do not attempt to depict the city as merely a larger medieval castle.

Use:

- impossible luminous architecture
- immense depth
- living light
- music integrated with environment
- transparent gold and pearl-like materials
- flowing water
- gardens
- crowds suggested through layered silhouettes
- awe through scale and restraint

### Final interaction

The player places the completed journey journal into a great library or receives a final page bearing the game’s completion record.

---

# Epilogue — Ignorance at the Gate

### Original story event

Ignorance reaches the river by another route and arrives without the required certificate. He is not admitted.

### Presentation

Treat this as a brief solemn epilogue after Christian’s entrance.

Do not make it a playable punishment scene.

### Purpose

Preserve the book’s warning that reaching the gate outwardly is not the same as being prepared to enter.

# Character Direction

## Christian

### Arc

- frightened
- burdened
- sincere but inexperienced
- repeatedly corrected
- equipped
- tested
- companioned
- discouraged
- restored
- hopeful
- welcomed

### Important adaptation rule

Christian is not a flawless hero. He makes poor choices, falls asleep, leads Hopeful into danger, becomes afraid, and needs correction.

---

## Faithful

### Role

A courageous companion whose route differs from Christian’s and whose witness affects Hopeful.

### Visual identity

- lean silhouette
- weathered clothing
- direct gaze
- warm but restrained colors
- movement lighter than Christian’s

---

## Hopeful

### Role

A companion born from what happened at Vanity Fair.

### Personality

- emotionally perceptive
- encouraging
- sometimes persuadable
- increasingly steady
- essential during the river crossing

---

## Evangelist

### Role

Appears at major directional and warning moments.

### Visual identity

- recognizable silhouette from a distance
- travel-worn cloak
- book or scroll
- calm, firm voice
- no magical wizard presentation

---

## Interpreter

### Role

Teacher through images and experiences.

### Visual identity

- composed
- candlelit
- keys or ring of room tokens
- garments that echo the house’s colors

---

# Story Adaptation Rules

1. Keep Christian’s journey and major encounters in the original order.
2. Preserve the difference between Christian, Faithful, and Hopeful.
3. Preserve Christian’s mistakes.
4. Do not transform every spiritual conflict into physical combat.
5. Use environmental mechanics to embody allegory.
6. Give major opposing characters persuasive dialogue.
7. Present explanations after the player experiences the symbol when possible.
8. Use concise original quotations sparingly.
9. Mark direct Bunyan quotations in content data.
10. Write original modern-language dialogue for gameplay.
11. Do not invent collectible currencies that conflict with the story’s themes.
12. Reward attentiveness, courage, patience, compassion, and discernment.
13. Do not create a morality score that reduces the story to points.
14. Let optional study content provide biblical references without blocking play.
15. End each level with a short visual recap and map progression.
